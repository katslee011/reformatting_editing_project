# Reflection
For WRIT 4999, Project 3,
I created three documents that demonstrate my ability to
use Markdown, use HTML, and edit other people's work.

## Assessment
In the approved project proposal,
I agreed to assess my project on six criterion:

* Collaboration and relationship building
* Critical thinking and problem solving
* Initiative and self-direction
* Quality of reformatting
* Quality of editing
* Summary of changes

After considering the various success and circumstances around each criterion,
I believe I've earned **96/100 points** on this assignment.

### Collaboration and Relationship Building
During the individual meeting,
I mentioned my concerns about meeting this criterion.
Following instructor feedback,
I decided to change my original plan to reformat and edit a document using XML during the third week
and instead find a document created by *someone else* to edit.
I succeeded a document written by someone else,
Professor Hall's article for Technically We Write,
and I successfully collaborated with him to edit his article.

Because of my success,
I believe I've earned **10/10 points** for this criterion.

### Critical Thinking and Problem Solving
While most project was smooth sailing,
I encountered unexpected difficulties in converting Document 2 into HTML.
In retrospect,
I should have realized that HTML doesn't automatically created numbered and lettered lists like Google Docs does.
Because HTML doesn't,
I had to figure out how to use HTML to create lettered lists
(HTML automatically assumes ordered lists are numerated).
Additionally,
I knew that colored text wasn't accessible,
and while I guessed CSS would let me differentiate the text some other way,
I didn't actually know how.

To solve both problems,
I went to Google.
Specifically,
I went to [W3Schools](https://www.w3schools.com/),
which I knew had a lot of information on both HTML and CSS.
In the end,
I found the solution to both my problems,
so I consider this criterion a success:
**10/10 points**.

### Initiative and Self-Direction
I believe I demonstrated a lot of *initiative* for this project,
but I demonstrated less *self-direction*.
For initiative,
I contacted the people I needed to contact,
I sought out the knowledge I needed to posses,
I found the tools I needed to use.
Ultimately,
no matter what,
I always knew what I needed to do next.
For self-direction,
while I began strong,
after I encountered unexpected difficulties for Document 2,
I began falling behind on my agreed-upon timeline.

Despite my failure, I think I need to give myself a little grace.
I hadn't originally included the Summary of Changes in either my timeline or criteria,
so the addition of three documents to my load might have exacerbated issues.
Overall,
I think I've earned **8/10** points for this criterion.

### Quality of Reformatting
While Document 1 demonstrates less Markdown than I'd hoped,
Document 2 demonstrates more than I anticipated.
In the description of this criterion,
I'd specifically mentioned the documents should demonstrate
*understanding of italics and bolding*.
Document 1 lacks an example of bold text,
and Document 2 lacks an example of italic text.
However,
to create Document 2,
I had to learn more HTML
(`type` and `text-decoration` attributes)
so ultimately,
Document 2 demonstrates a *greater* quality of reformatting than I'd intended.
Additionally,
I met the other parts of the criterion
(headings, alt-text, semantic HTML).

Because of those reasons,
I think I've earned **23/25** points on this criterion.

### Quality of Editing
I believe my work is free of spelling and grammar errors,
and I believe I've edited it to be as clear as possible,
but ultimately,
I can't be an objective judge of my writing.
At least, for Document 3,
my 'client' (Professor Jim Hall)
was satisfied with my work.
All my documents (Documents 1 and 2)
followed the APA guide to the extent allowed by formatting
(APA was not intended to act as a style guide for websites).

I believe the focus of this criterion should be Document 3,
the document dedicated solely to editing in a real-world context,
and because that part of the assignment was a success,
I believe I've earned **25/25** points on this criterion.

### Summary of Changes
Like with the previous criterion,
it's difficult to be objective about the quality of my writing.
However, I believe I landed on an effective structure for my summaries
(the original versions were just paragraphs),
and I believe I provided sound justifications for my changes.

I believe I've earned **20/20** points for this criterion.

## Experience
Looking back on my writing and comparing it to my resume,
I realized that I actually have a lot of examples of my writing ability,
but I lack good examples of my other hard skills.
While I have documents I created for the Writing with Digital Technology class,
I realized that they weren't good examples of my *writing*,
and while I have editing knowledge from Editing, Style, & Critique,
I don't have a lot of good *examples*.

Technically,
I could have used any of the documents I created for those classes and called it a day,
but I believe a portfolio should consist of as few documents as possible.
Recruiters are unlikely to spend very long on any of my documents.
If they see that I claim to be good at both writing and HTML,
and they need me to be good at both,
they might choose to look at my HTML examples only.
If my HTML example(s) have poor writing,
or if I lack an HTML example in my portfolio,
I risk ruining my chances.
Ideally,
my examples of Markdown and/or HTML should demonstrate
both a good grasp on the markup language *and* a strong writing ability.

Because of my thought process,
I focused on *quality* for my criterion.
Originally,
I included a criterion on completion because I wouldn't complete all the documents otherwise,
but after feedback,
I changed it to Summary of Changes,
which I thought about including because I realized the professor of this class didn't have access to the original documents.

I used Google Calendar to manage my deadlines because I use Google Calendar to manage all my deadlines.
As the end of the semester caught up to me, however,
this method began to fail.

I worked with Professor Jim Hall to complete this project.
I reached out to him through email,
then we continued to communicate through email,
and then he sent over the article draft.
I completed my edits through Google Docs suggestions and comments
and sent the edited article back to him by the agreed deadline
(according to the timeline in the proposal plan, replacing the XML document).

The most intersting aspect of this project was definitely Document 2,
but the most valuable part was Document 3.
I learned more about HTML and CSS than I expected,
and generally,
I enjoy using HTML and CSS.
I might do more personal exploration into HTML and CSS's capabilities in my free time.
However, no matter how fun HTML and CSS are,
they still felt isolated from a real-world context,
unlike the work I did for Document 3.
Working in a real capacity,
even if for free,
gave me more confidence in my abilities.

## Learning
As mentioned throughout the reflection,
I learned more about HTML and CSS than I expected.
My project demonstrates this directly,
as I had to use the HTML and CSS I learned to complete Document 2.
Additionally,
I used HTML to create the website [WRIT 4999 Project 3](https://katslee011.github.io/reformatting_editing_project/),
and for that website,

(which I did for links to outside websites).

I learned that I *do* know how to edit,

I hadn't applied it in a real-world context before,
and until I apply it in a real-world context,
I feel unsure about my skills.

and if I'd known how much time they'd take, I'd remove Document 1, the Markdown example.
Although Markdown is valuable,
it's simple,
and I think focusing my energy into HTML and editing would've been more worthwhile.
